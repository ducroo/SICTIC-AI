# Additional team assessment questions

New questions use the identifier sequence **N001, N002, ...**. Reference checklist areas use **R001, R002, ...**. Existing question identifiers remain unchanged.

For phase-aware probes, use **Insufficient information** when a relevant item cannot be established from the available evidence. Use **Not applicable** only when the item genuinely does not apply to the startup. Missing due-diligence documents at application stage are not negative evidence; preserve them as structured follow-up items.

## N001 — Founder traits assessment

**Category:** 1.1 Demonstrated capability and track record

For each active founder, assess the strength of factual evidence for the following five traits:

1. **Drive and agency:** High operating pace, rapid progression, concurrent achievements, self-initiated projects, resourcefulness, and action without requiring substantial institutional support.
2. **Cognitive ability and learning:** Demanding or selective education, complex technical or analytical responsibilities, intellectual achievements, technical depth, and evidence of learning difficult subjects.
3. **Leadership and interpersonal effectiveness:** Leadership responsibility, people management, recruiting, stakeholder influence, communication, and repeated positions of trust.
4. **Grit and follow-through:** Sustained performance in demanding environments, completion of difficult projects, long-term commitment, repeated iteration, and persistence through visible setbacks.
5. **Adaptability and range:** Successful work across functions, roles, domains, organizations, countries, or materially different operating environments.

### Evidence score

- **5 — Exceptional:** Multiple strong and independently meaningful signals.
- **4 — Strong:** Clear and substantial evidence.
- **3 — Moderate:** Some credible evidence, but limited depth or repetition.
- **2 — Weak:** Only indirect or ambiguous signals.
- **1 — Contrary:** Available evidence meaningfully suggests the trait is lacking.
- **N/A — Not assessable:** Insufficient evidence; do not interpret missing information as negative evidence.

For every score, provide the specific facts supporting it. Assess each founder separately and do not calculate a combined or average trait score at this stage. Titles alone should not be treated as conclusive evidence.

## N002 — Founder role readiness

**Category:** 1.2 Fit with role and priorities

For each active founder:

1. Identify their stated role and the most important responsibilities that role entails in this specific pre-seed startup.
2. Compare those responsibilities with the founder's demonstrated experience and capabilities.
3. Provide:
   - **Foundation:** Summarize the experience, skills, and achievements that support the founder's readiness for the role. Include relevant transferable experience, even if they have not previously held the same title. Use 2–3 sentences.
   - **Shortfalls:** Summarize material gaps, mismatches, or areas in which the founder has not yet demonstrated readiness for the role. Distinguish an identifiable shortfall from information that is simply unavailable. Use 2–3 sentences.
   - **Role-readiness score:** Balance the foundation and shortfalls using the scale below.
   - **Confidence:** State whether confidence is high, moderate, or low based on the amount and specificity of available evidence.

### Role-readiness score

- **5 — Highly ready:** Strong, directly relevant evidence across nearly all critical responsibilities.
- **4 — Ready:** Strong relevant foundation with limited or manageable shortfalls.
- **3 — Partially ready:** Credible transferable foundation, but one or more material responsibilities remain unproven.
- **2 — Weakly ready:** Limited relevant foundation or substantial mismatch with the role.
- **1 — Not ready:** Available evidence indicates a fundamental mismatch with the role.
- **N/A — Not assessable:** The available information is insufficient to form a responsible assessment.

Do not assume that a first-time CEO or CTO is unqualified merely because they have not held that title before. Assess the underlying responsibilities and transferable evidence, not title repetition.

## N003 — Founder commitment and personal exposure

**Category:** 1.3 Commitment, availability and alignment

What personal resources and opportunity costs have the active founders committed to the startup before the proposed financing?

For each founder, report any stated evidence of:

- Cash invested, shareholder loans, or personal guarantees.
- Unpaid or below-market work.
- Foregone employment or other professional opportunities.
- Personally contributed IP, equipment, or other material resources.
- Other meaningful personal exposure.

State disclosed cash amounts in CHF and the timing of the commitment where available. Distinguish amounts already committed from amounts merely promised.

Do not assume that lack of disclosed personal investment means no investment was made. Use **N/A — Not assessable** where the dossier provides insufficient information. Emphasize whether founders have assumed meaningful exposure and opportunity cost; do not reward founders simply for being wealthy enough to invest more cash.

## N004 — Duration of commitment and progress velocity

**Category:** 1.3 Commitment, availability and alignment

How long has each active founder been working on the startup, how much of that time has been full-time, and is the progress achieved proportionate to the elapsed time?

Report:

- When work on the startup began.
- When each founder joined.
- When each founder became full-time, if applicable.
- Approximate founder-months of full-time and part-time effort.
- Material milestones achieved during that period.
- Evidence of product, customer, commercial, or financing progress.

Conclude whether the available evidence indicates:

- **Too early to establish commitment:** The founders have worked on it for too little time to demonstrate sustained commitment.
- **Proportionate progress:** The duration and achieved milestones appear reasonable for this startup's type and stage.
- **Extended effort with limited progress:** The founders have worked on it for a substantial period without evidence of progress proportionate to that time.
- **N/A — Not assessable:** Start dates, working intensity, or milestones cannot be established.

Do not apply a universal month threshold. Compare elapsed founder effort with the difficulty of the work, the startup type, and the milestones achieved.

## N005 — Essential functional coverage

**Category:** 2.2 Functional coverage and gap-closing

Which functions are essential for this startup's next stage, and does the founding team have demonstrated capability and clear ownership for each one?

Assess at least:

- **Product, technical, and domain execution.**
- **Go-to-market:** Customer definition, positioning, pricing, and acquisition strategy.
- **Sales:** Prospecting, pipeline development, negotiation, and closing.
- **Operations:** Delivery of the product or service in practice.
- **Venture-specific functions:** For example regulatory, clinical, security, certification, or marketplace operations.

For hardware startups, explicitly assess founder-level understanding and ownership of:

- Design for manufacturing.
- Supplier identification and management.
- Procurement and component availability.
- Production cost and bill of materials.
- Quality control.
- Logistics.
- Certification and production scale-up.

For each essential function, report:

- Its importance at the startup's present stage.
- The responsible founder.
- Specific evidence of that founder's capability.
- The resulting coverage assessment.

Use:

- **Covered:** Clear ownership and demonstrated capability.
- **Partially covered:** Named ownership with limited or transferable evidence.
- **Uncovered:** No founder appears capable of owning the function.
- **N/A:** The function is not important at the startup's present stage or for its model.

A named role alone does not establish functional coverage.

## N006 — Founder allocation to non-core functions

**Category:** 2.2 Functional coverage and gap-closing

Is a material share of the founding team dedicated primarily to HR, finance, legal, administration, or another support function that does not require founder-level ownership at this stage?

Identify:

- Founders whose primary role is a support function.
- Whether that role fills an immediate, material execution need.
- Which core product, commercial, or operational capability is consequently missing.
- Whether the function could reasonably be handled through external support or a limited part-time responsibility.

Treat dedicated HR, finance, legal, and administrative founder roles as a concern for a pre-seed startup unless the function is integral to the product or principal execution risk. Possible exceptions include regulated financial infrastructure, legal technology, or a venture whose core execution depends on specialist regulatory capability.

## Reference checklist areas

### R001 — Organisational structure

**Category:** 2.1 Dynamics and cohesion

Probe the current organisational structure, including active founders, employees, reporting relationships, and open positions.

- **Pitch application:** Reconstruct a simple team map from the dossier and public sources.
- **Due diligence:** Confirm it against the formal organisation chart and employment records.

### R002 — Roles, responsibilities, and work locations

**Primary category:** 1.2 Fit with role and priorities  
**Secondary category:** 1.3 Commitment, availability and alignment

Probe the roles, responsibilities, decision ownership, workload, and work location of each key team member.

- **Pitch application:** Extract the information from the application, deck, LinkedIn, website, and other available sources.
- **Due diligence:** Confirm it against contracts and actual working arrangements.

### R003 — CVs and supporting credentials

**Category:** 1.1 Demonstrated capability and track record

Probe claimed education, qualifications, and work experience, including available supporting certificates and employment references.

- **Pitch application:** Assess CV, LinkedIn, and public evidence. Report unavailable certificates or work reports as **Insufficient information**.
- **Due diligence:** Verify material claims against certificates, references, and work reports.

### R004 — Employment arrangements

**Primary category:** 3.1 Ownership, control and formal agreements  
**Secondary category:** 1.3 Commitment, availability and alignment

Probe employment arrangements for founders and employees, including whether agreements exist and whether material terms are documented.

- **Pitch application:** Report disclosed employment status and conflicting obligations; otherwise use **Insufficient information**.
- **Due diligence:** Review written or oral agreements, time commitment, compensation, notice, IP assignment, and restrictive obligations.

### R005 — Identity verification

**Category:** 4.3 Identity, public standing and adverse information

Probe identity verification for founders and other relevant team members.

- **Pitch application:** Check consistency of public identity information. Direct proof may be unavailable.
- **Due diligence:** Verify against passports or identification cards.

### R006 — Debt-enforcement records

**Category:** 4.3 Identity, public standing and adverse information

Probe debt-enforcement history and the availability of current Betreibungsregister excerpts.

- **Pitch application:** Search for publicly checkable indications. Formal excerpts will usually be unavailable.
- **Due diligence:** Review current official excerpts and investigate disclosed entries.

### R007 — Criminal-record information

**Category:** 4.3 Identity, public standing and adverse information

Probe criminal-record information relevant to founder integrity or the ability to operate the venture.

- **Pitch application:** Use only lawfully available public information. Official records will normally be unavailable.
- **Due diligence:** Review official extracts where requested and appropriate.

### R008 — ESOP/PSOP

**Category:** 3.1 Ownership, control and formal agreements

Probe any existing or planned ESOP/PSOP, including approvals, grants, vesting terms, the available pool, and resulting dilution.

- **Pitch application:** Report what is disclosed and whether the financing plan assumes an option pool.
- **Due diligence:** Verify plan documents, approvals, grants, cap-table treatment, and the remaining pool.

### R009 — Performance management

**Category:** 2.3 Leadership, power balance and resilience

Probe whether performance-management and goal-setting practices are appropriate to the team's current size and maturity.

- **Pitch application:** Usually **Not applicable** to a founder-only team; do not expect formal OKRs or peer reviews.
- **Due diligence:** Assess once the company has employees or meaningful organisational complexity.

### R010 — Hiring process

**Category:** 2.2 Functional coverage and gap-closing

Probe the approach to identifying, attracting, selecting, and onboarding critical hires.

- **Pitch application:** Focus on planned roles, timing, required profiles, cost, and access to candidates.
- **Due diligence:** Review the actual hiring process, candidate pipeline, decision responsibilities, and employment compliance.

## Notes on existing questions

### Q011 — Principal execution risk

**Question:** Does at least one active founder have expertise for the startup's principal execution risk?

**Definition required:** A robust definition and identification method for the startup's **principal execution risk** must be developed before Q011 can be applied consistently. The assessment needs to distinguish the most important execution risk from general startup risks and explain how that risk is derived from the startup's business, product, market, stage, and immediate priorities.

### Q014 — Founder salaries funded by the raise

**Question:** Is the startup pre-revenue while founder salaries are high and funded mostly by the fresh raise?

**Evidence limitation:** This information will rarely be available from the submitted dossier, LinkedIn profiles, the startup's website, or other public sources. Use **N/A — Not assessable** when founder salaries and their funding source are not explicitly disclosed; do not infer them from the fundraising amount or financial projections alone.

### Q003 — Romantic or family relationships among founders

**Current question:** Are all named founders exclusively a romantic couple, with no independent team member?

**Evidence limitation:** Romantic and family relationships will often be very difficult to determine reliably from the submitted dossier, LinkedIn profiles, the startup's website, and other public information. Use **N/A — Not assessable** rather than inferring a relationship from names, photographs, living locations, or personal familiarity.

**Suggested wording:** Are there romantic couples or family relationships among the founders?

### Q004 — Personal-relationship risk

**Question:** Does the team explicitly address the personal-relationship risk?

**Overlap note:** This covers the same underlying issue as Q003 and should be considered together with it rather than treated as a separate assessment topic.

### Q016 — Shared work history

**Question:** Do the founders share a long prior work history together at one existing company?

**Overlap note:** This is a specific version of the broader Q027, which asks whether the founders have a long, pre-existing shared history.

### Q029 — Assessability of interpersonal fit

**Question:** Can interpersonal team-dynamics fit be assessed from any available source?

**Question-design note:** This is not itself a substantive assessment question. Q003 is the substantive question in this area; Q029 instead describes whether the available evidence permits an assessment.

### Q131 — Advisors or board members offsetting a team gap

**Question:** Where a team gap is shown, is there any advisor/board presence that could offset it?

**Interpretation note:** An advisor or board member may reduce a knowledge gap, but normally cannot replace active founder ownership of sales, customer discovery, product delivery, or supplier management. External support should not automatically cause an essential function to be classified as covered.

### Q117 — Majority equity and operational title

**Question:** Does the majority-equity founder also hold the operational decision-making title?

**Question-design note:** The current formulation is not sufficiently decision-relevant for pre-seed screening. SICTIC does not necessarily care whether majority equity and the operational title sit with the same person. Sharpen the question around a concrete risk or remove it.

### Q143 — Primary-founder absence

**Question:** What would break if the primary founder were unreachable for 60–90 days?

**Question-design note:** The hypothetical produces an obvious problem for almost every pre-seed startup and therefore has little discriminatory value. Sharpen it to identify abnormal concentration of execution or remove it.

### Q144 — Unilateral control and succession planning

**Question:** Does the founder's voting/control structure allow unilateral decisions, and is there a succession plan?

**Stage-fit note:** A formal succession plan is generally too early to expect at the pre-seed screening stage. The unilateral voting and control element may still be relevant and should be assessed separately from succession planning.

### Q129 — Relevant board and advisor expertise

**Question:** Does any board member or advisor have direct, verifiable domain expertise in the core technical claim?

**Interpretation note:** Relevant board or advisor value is not limited to expertise in the core technical claim. Board members and advisors should contribute demonstrable expertise in at least one material area such as the startup's industry, go-to-market, sales, scaling, or manufacturing.

### Q132 — Advisor/customer comparison

**Current question:** Does the team slide list more named advisors than referenceable customers?

**Question-design note:** Confusing question. Rephrase it.

**Evidence limitation:** This information will be in neither the Dealum dossier nor the data room. The agent will therefore always answer **Insufficient information**.

**Evidence limitation:** This information will be in neither the Dealum dossier nor the data room. The agent will therefore always answer **Insufficient information**.

### Q139 — Betreibungsregister excerpts and criminal records

**Overlap note:** This is a further specification of Q136, which asks whether there is any publicly checkable indication of outstanding enforcement proceedings.
