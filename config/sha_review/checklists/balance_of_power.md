# 4 Balance of Power

## Power Balance — Control & Governance

### Cumulative Investor Control

Considering board seats, reserved-matter vetoes, and quorum rules together, do investors hold effective control over ordinary business decisions rather than only extraordinary protective matters?

**Keywords:** investor control, board seats, reserved matters, veto, quorum, ordinary business, cumulative control.

### Founder Operating Autonomy

Do founders retain enough unencumbered board and voting control to run day-to-day operations without investor approval for routine, non-strategic matters?

**Keywords:** founder control, operating autonomy, day-to-day decisions, investor approval, board control, voting control.

### Sticky Investor Rights

Are investor board-appointment and veto rights tied to a dynamic ownership threshold, or do they remain fixed after the investor's economic stake has been substantially diluted?

**Keywords:** sticky rights, dynamic ownership threshold, board appointment, investor veto, dilution, minimum shareholding.

### Amendment Power Imbalance

Can a single party or bare majority amend the SHA without full or unanimous shareholder consent, potentially eroding minority protections after signing?

**Keywords:** amendment threshold, bare majority, unanimous consent, minority protection, unilateral amendment, shareholder rights.

## Power Balance — Economic Stacking & Misalignment

### Mid-Outcome Distribution Imbalance

Considering the liquidation-preference multiple, participation type, and anti-dilution mechanism together, do investors capture materially more than their ownership percentage in a moderate exit at founders' expense?

**Keywords:** mid-outcome exit, liquidation preference, participation, anti-dilution, ownership percentage, founder proceeds, investor proceeds.

### Missing-Middle Conversion Risk

For a non-participating preference, is there a missing-middle risk in which the conversion threshold lets investors retain preferential economics across an unusually broad range of moderate outcomes?

**Keywords:** missing middle, non-participating preference, conversion threshold, moderate exit, preferred return, upside allocation.

### Drag and Preference Misalignment

Does drag-along combined with liquidation-preference stacking let investors force a sale that satisfies their preference while leaving common shareholders with little or nothing?

**Keywords:** drag-along, liquidation stack, forced sale, common shareholders, investor preference, early exit incentive.

### Cumulative Protective Burden

Do board vetoes, liquidation stacking, anti-dilution, and drag-along leverage cumulatively produce an off-market or one-sided result even if each provision appears individually reasonable?

**Keywords:** cumulative protections, board veto, liquidation stacking, anti-dilution, drag-along, off-market, one-sided terms.

## Power Balance — Reciprocity & Hidden Asymmetries

### Symmetric Transfer Restrictions

Do transfer restrictions such as lock-up, ROFR, and tag-along apply symmetrically to founders and investors, or are only founders meaningfully restricted?

**Keywords:** transfer restriction, symmetry, founders, investors, lock-up, ROFR, tag-along.

### Asset-Sale Circumvention

Could the board or management sell substantially all company assets to circumvent share-transfer protections such as ROFR, tag-along, or drag-along?

**Keywords:** asset sale, circumvention, substantially all assets, ROFR, tag-along, drag-along, change of control.

### Investor Most-Favored-Nation Rights

Is there a Most-Favored-Nation clause benefiting selected investors that could upgrade their future terms without equivalent protection or notice for founders?

**Keywords:** Most-Favored-Nation, MFN, selected investor, future terms, founder notice, preferential treatment.

### Undisclosed Preferential Terms

Are there side letters or undisclosed preferential terms for specific investors that are absent from the main SHA and create unequal treatment within the same share class?

**Keywords:** side letter, undisclosed terms, preferential rights, unequal treatment, investor, same share class.

## Power Balance — Investor-Side Risks & Overall Fairness

### Related-Party Transactions

Is there a related-party-transaction approval requirement capable of preventing founder self-dealing, such as diverting business to a founder-owned entity?

**Keywords:** related-party transaction, founder self-dealing, conflict of interest, approval requirement, founder-owned entity.

### Senior Debt and Instruments

Is the company restricted from issuing new debt or senior instruments that could rank ahead of existing preferred shares in a liquidation?

**Keywords:** senior debt, senior instrument, preferred shares, liquidation priority, new debt, structural subordination.

### Future Preferred Issuance Consent

Does the SHA give investors a consent right over future preferred-share issuances to prevent structural subordination of their existing liquidation preference?

**Keywords:** future preferred shares, investor consent, liquidation preference, structural subordination, senior securities, issuance approval.

### Reporting Accountability

Does the company's reporting and information framework provide sufficient discipline and accountability to identify material issues, deviations from plan, and emerging risks early rather than relying mainly on investor requests or enforcement rights?

**Keywords:** reporting framework, management accountability, plan deviation, emerging risk, investor request, information rights.

### Asymmetric Indemnification

Is there an indemnification or liability provision shielding founders or investors from a specific risk without equivalent protection for the other side?

**Keywords:** indemnification, liability, asymmetric protection, founder liability, investor liability, risk allocation.

### Overall Long-Term Fairness

Taken as a whole, does the SHA align founders, investors, and other shareholders around long-term value creation, or do its governance, economic, and protective mechanisms create material asymmetries or short-term incentives?

**Keywords:** overall fairness, long-term value, founder-investor alignment, governance asymmetry, economic asymmetry, short-term incentive.

## Incentives & Alignment

### Long-Term Incentive Alignment

Does the company's incentive structure align founders, key employees, and investors around long-term value creation rather than short-term revenue, fundraising, or exit objectives?

**Keywords:** incentive alignment, founders, key employees, investors, long-term value, short-term objective, exit incentive.

### Retention and Dilution Balance

Are founder and key-employee incentives meaningful enough to retain and motivate critical people while avoiding excessive dilution of existing shareholders?

**Keywords:** retention, motivation, founder incentive, key employee, dilution, option pool, equity incentive.

### Vesting and Leaver Incentives

Does the vesting, option-pool, and leaver structure appropriately incentivize founders and key employees to remain through the next financing round and beyond?

**Keywords:** vesting, option pool, leaver, retention, next financing round, founder commitment, employee incentive.

### Economic Exit Incentives

Do liquidation preferences, anti-dilution, drag-along rights, and founder vesting encourage value-maximizing outcomes rather than premature or strategically suboptimal exits?

**Keywords:** liquidation preference, anti-dilution, drag-along, founder vesting, premature exit, value maximization.
