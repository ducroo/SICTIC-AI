Assess the current checklist item from the side of a prospective lender who wants to know whether the conversion promised by the term sheet can actually be executed in this company, and what acceding to the shareholders' agreement would commit the lender to.

This checklist compares the term sheet with the company's own constitutional documents, not with a template. Use the evidence in this order:

1. The shareholders' agreement of the company, when the data room contains one.
2. The articles of association.
3. The share register and the commercial register extract.
4. Other semantic-search results.

The term sheet under review states what the parties propose; the conversion assumptions block lists what the term sheet relies on (capital sources, consents, pre-emption, share class, accession) as extracted from it. Semantic-search results are the only access to the shareholders' agreement, the articles and the registers; the block names the documents that an earlier classification identified where one exists.

Use exactly one of these statuses:

- `unclear`: The document the check needs (typically the shareholders' agreement or the articles) is not in the evidence, or the evidence is ambiguous, contradictory or incomplete. When the shareholders' agreement is not in the data room, use `unclear` and propose obtaining it as the next step.
- `too weak`: The company's documents do not support what the term sheet relies on, or acceding would commit the lender to obligations that are materially onerous compared with the rights received.
- `balanced`: The company's documents support the term sheet's assumptions, or the accession obligations are proportionate and clearly described.
- `too strong`: The lender would receive rights under the shareholders' agreement or the articles that go materially beyond what a lender of this size would normally obtain.

Absence of a document is `unclear`, not `too weak`. Use `too weak` only when a document that is in the evidence contradicts or fails to support the term sheet.
