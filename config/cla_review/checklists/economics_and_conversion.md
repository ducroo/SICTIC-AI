# 1 Economics and Conversion

## Conversion price

### Cap and discount interplay

Does the term sheet state both a valuation cap and a discount, and does it make clear that the lender converts at the lower of the capped price and the discounted round price? As the lender, check whether either mechanism is missing, whether one is defined in a way that can never bind, and whether the SECA reference expresses the same "lower of" logic.

**Keywords:** valuation cap, discount, conversion price, lower of, cap price, discounted price.

### Cap definition and denominator

Is the valuation cap defined as a pre-money or post-money figure, does it state whether the converting loans are included, and does it say which shares form the denominator (fully diluted including option pools and other convertibles, or issued shares only)? An undefined denominator lets the company choose the reading that gives the lender fewer shares.

**Keywords:** pre-money, post-money, fully diluted, issued shares, denominator, option pool, ESOP, share count.

### Discount level and schedule

Is the discount fixed for the whole term or does it rise with the time the loan is outstanding? Compare the discount with the loan term: a flat discount on a long loan under-compensates the lender for the extra time at risk, and the SECA drafting notes describe a rising schedule as the usual answer.

**Keywords:** discount schedule, step-up, time-based discount, loan term, months outstanding, flat discount.

### Valuation floor

Does the term sheet impose a valuation floor or minimum conversion price that limits the effect of the discount for the lender? A floor protects the company and existing shareholders; from the lender's side it is a concession that needs a clear justification.

**Keywords:** valuation floor, minimum conversion price, floor price, minimum valuation.

## Conversion events

### Qualified financing round threshold

Is the qualified equity financing round defined by a minimum amount that is realistic for the company's stage, and does the minimum count new money only or also the converting loans? Counting the loans lets a small round trigger a mandatory conversion; the lender wants the threshold to describe fresh capital from new investors.

**Keywords:** qualified financing round, QEFR, minimum round size, new money, gross proceeds, mandatory conversion, threshold.

### Non-qualified round and voluntary conversion

If a financing round below the threshold takes place, may the lender (or a lender majority) elect to convert on the same economics, and what happens to the loan otherwise? A term sheet that only converts on a qualified round leaves the lender as a creditor while new investors take equity in a smaller round.

**Keywords:** non-qualified round, voluntary conversion, election to convert, lender option, small round, optional conversion.

### Maturity mechanism and price

What happens at maturity if no qualifying round has occurred: repayment, conversion at the cap, conversion at a stated or negotiated valuation, or an extension? Who decides, and is the conversion price at maturity defined without reference to a round that has not happened? A missing maturity mechanism leaves the lender with a repayment claim against a company that usually cannot pay.

**Keywords:** maturity, maturity conversion, repayment at maturity, extension, maturity valuation, conversion at cap.

### Change of control

On a sale of the company before conversion, does the lender receive a repayment multiple, a conversion at the cap immediately before the sale, or the better of the two at the lender's election? Compare the multiple and the election right with the SECA reference.

**Keywords:** change of control, exit, sale of the company, repayment multiple, liquidity event, acceleration on exit.

## Interest

### Interest rate, basis and conversion of interest

Is the interest rate stated together with its day-count basis and whether it is simple or compounding, and does accrued interest convert into shares together with the principal? Check the rate against a Swiss safe-harbor reference where the term sheet mentions one, and whether interest is paid in cash or only accrued.

**Keywords:** interest rate, accrued interest, day count, simple interest, compound interest, safe harbor, interest conversion, interest payment.
