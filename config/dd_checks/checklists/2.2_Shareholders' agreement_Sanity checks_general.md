# 100 Sanity-Check Questions for Due Diligence on a Shareholders' Agreement (SHA)

Scoped strictly to the SHA. Synthesizes: SICTIC handbook notes on contract law and preferred shares, the SECA/Baker McKenzie SHA training material, the official SECA 5th Edition (Large) Model SHA clause structure, the Swiss Angel Investor Handbook, and the SICTIC webinars. The final section (83–100) looks across clauses for founder/investor power balance, one-sidedness, and cumulative risk — effects that individual clause-by-clause checks can miss.

## A. Existence, Validity & Document Integrity

1. Does a single, current, signed SHA actually exist — with no shareholders still bound by an older, unreplaced version?
2. Are there any amendments, side letters, or addenda not consolidated into the current version?
3. Have **all** current shareholders of record signed it, with no gaps against the Cap Table?
4. Do the share classes, percentages, and instruments named in the SHA match the current Cap Table exactly?
5. Is the SHA consistent with the Articles?
6. Is the **Company itself** a party to the SHA?
7. Does the SHA state clearly which document prevails in case of conflict — SHA, Articles, or Board Regulations?
8. Are all capitalized terms used throughout the SHA actually defined in the Definitions annex, avoiding ambiguity in interpretation?
9. Does the Entire Agreement clause properly supersede the term sheet and any prior negotiations, avoiding later disputes over "what was actually agreed"?
10. Is there a severability clause ensuring one unenforceable provision doesn't void the entire SHA?
11. Does the Notices clause specify valid delivery methods and addresses (including whether email is acceptable) for formal communications?

## B. Ownership Structure & Cap Table Consistency

12. Does the SHA's Ownership Structure section accurately reflect the cap table immediately following completion of this financing round, not a stale draft version?
13. If new share classes are being implemented, is this consistently cross-referenced with the Articles' authorized or conditional capital provisions?
14. Does the SHA clearly state the total number of shares, nominal value, and share-class breakdown post-transaction, matching the Cap Table and Articles annexes?

## C. Governance & Board

15. How many board seats exist, and who has the right to nominate each one?
16. Do investor board-appointment rights depend on a minimum ownership threshold — and is that threshold still met today?
17. Is there a defined list of Important Shareholder Matters requiring supermajority or investor consent?
18. Is there a separate list of Important Board Matters requiring investor-director consent?
19. Are quorum requirements set at a level any single party could block simply by not attending?
20. Is there a stepped-down quorum mechanism for a reconvened meeting, to prevent deadlock-by-absence?
21. Is there a deadlock-resolution mechanism (casting vote, mediation, buy-sell clause)?
22. Are board observers explicitly distinguished from directors, with no vote and clear confidentiality obligations?
23. Does the SHA's signing authority clause match what's actually registered in the Commercial Register?

## D. Information Rights

24. Does the company have a disciplined management-reporting process that gives the CEO and the Board timely visibility over financial performance, cash position, key operating KPIs and progress against budget, regardless of whether the formal quarterly shareholder report is occasionally delayed by higher-priority business needs?
25. Is a minimum ownership threshold required to trigger these information rights?
26. If the SHA is silent, are shareholders relying only on the statutory minimum (annual report once a year, up to 18 months' delay)?
27. Is there a right for investors to appoint an independent auditor to inspect the books, and who bears the cost?
28. Is there a right to request ad hoc information or regular discussions with management?

## E. Company Policies, Financial Governance & Funding Commitments

29. Are accounting standards and policies specified (e.g. Swiss GAAP FER, IFRS), keeping financials comparable and audit-ready for future rounds?
30. Is the appointment and removal of auditors addressed, with auditor independence protected?
31. Is there an explicit "No Commitment for Further Funding" disclaimer, making clear no investor is obligated to participate in future rounds?
32. Does the SHA place any conditions or approval rights on how the company raises additional financing (e.g. consent required before external financing talks)?
33. Is there a Business Plan annex referenced, and does the reserved-matters list require consent for material deviations from it?
34. Are Company Policies (code of conduct, expense policy, etc.) addressed, with a clear board-approval process for future changes?
35. Are KYC/AML compliance obligations placed on shareholders (e.g. providing beneficial-ownership documentation) as a condition of the SHA's validity?

## F. Economic Rights — Dividends & Liquidation Preference

36. Does the SHA set any dividend policy, or is distribution left entirely to GM discretion?
37. If PE/VC investors are involved, are dividends restricted for an initial period, and is that period clearly defined (not left as a vague "future catch-up")?
38. Is a dividend preference clearly separated from liquidation preference (they're often confused)?
39. What is the liquidation preference multiple, and is it fully participating (the "double-dip"), non-participating, or participating with a cap? (Swiss norm: 1x; 2x–4x+ is unusual here.)
40. If capped, is the cap multiple stated (Swiss norm: roughly 3x–5x) with a conversion-to-common option once proceeds exceed it?
41. Is the seniority/stacking order clear across multiple preferred rounds — does the most recent round rank senior and get paid out first?
42. Does the liquidation and dividend preference explicitly terminate upon an IPO?
43. Are the preference terms unusually harsh for the company's stage — a common sign of down-round investor leverage worth double-checking?

## G. Dilution & Subscription Rights

44. Do shareholders have pro-rata subscription/pre-emption rights for new share issuances?
45. Is there an anti-dilution clause, and is it full-ratchet or weighted-average (broad- or narrow-based)?
46. Does the anti-dilution protection have a sunset (e.g. expiring after the next round or after N years)?
47. Is anti-dilution protection subject to "pay-to-play" (only investors who join the down round stay protected)?
48. Have existing shareholders formally waived statutory subscription rights where needed for ESOP or anti-dilution top-ups?
49. Is a conversion mechanism (voluntary, and mandatory upon IPO) for preferred shares clearly specified, including any approval threshold required?

## H. Transfer Restrictions

50. Is there a general restriction confirming shares cannot be transferred, pledged, or encumbered except as permitted in the SHA?
51. Are "Permitted Transfers" (e.g. to affiliates, or ESOP-related transfers) clearly and narrowly scoped?
52. Is there a lock-up period, and has it already expired?
53. What is the transfer mechanism (ROFO, ROFR, both, or neither), and does it create a meaningful impediment to an eventual sale?
54. Does drag-along clearly take precedence over RoFR/RoFO and tag-along in case of conflict?
55. What threshold triggers drag-along, and is the purchase price required to be bona fide and in cash?
56. Does tag-along cover a genuine change-of-control trigger, obligating the buyer to extend the same terms to minority holders?
57. Could the RoFR provisions realistically deter a future acquirer, and is there an expiration or drag-along override addressing this?
58. Are shares expressly prohibited from being pledged as collateral, with any carve-out (e.g. VC fund-to-fund transfers) narrowly scoped?

## I. Leaver, Founder, Option Pool & Exit Provisions

59. Is there a founder vesting schedule (cliff + vesting period), and does it cover shares already held since incorporation?
60. Is there a Purchase Option covering triggering events (death, incapacity, insolvency, criminal act, bad/good leaver)?
61. Can investors/managers manufacture a Bad Leaver event?
62. Are good-leaver vs. bad-leaver pricing terms clearly defined (fair market value vs. nominal value)?
63. Is there an Investor Put Option (e.g. exit at symbolic value like CHF 1), and is it clear when it can be exercised?
64. Is there a clawback provision letting the company reclaim vested stock to replenish the option pool, with clearly bounded (not open-ended) conditions?
65. Are exit/IPO provisions addressed — market stand-off/lock-up in an IPO, and post-IPO registration rights?
66. Is there a defined "Preferred Exit" / "Qualified Exit" threshold determining when preference and conversion mechanics are triggered?

## J. Share Register, Accession & Release

67. Does the SHA confirm no physical share certificates are issued, consistent with how shares are actually tracked?
68. Is the process for registering new share issuances or transfers in the Share Register clearly defined?
69. Is there a formal Accession mechanism requiring new shareholders (including ESOP recipients) to sign onto the SHA?
70. Is there a corresponding Release mechanism formally discharging a shareholder from the SHA once they've fully exited?

## K. Duration & Termination

71. Is the duration and termination framework of the SHA appropriate for the company's expected lifecycle, with clear provisions defining when the agreement or specific shareholder rights cease to apply (e.g. upon an IPO, a shareholder's exit, a change of control or other specified events)?
72. Are ordinary and extraordinary termination grounds clearly spelled out?
73. Are the consequences of termination clear — does an exiting shareholder's stake get bought out, and at what price?
74. Is there a contractual penalty (liquidated damages) for breach, or would damages have to be proven in court?

## L. Miscellaneous, Confidentiality & Dispute Resolution

75. Is there a confidentiality clause binding all shareholders (not just the company), with reasonable carve-outs (e.g. disclosure to auditors or regulators)?
76. Is there a non-compete/non-solicitation clause binding founders, and is it enforceable under Swiss law (reasonable duration and geographic scope)?
77. Does the SHA indicate whether it qualifies as a simple partnership or a regular contract under Swiss law — since this affects whether claims must be brought jointly and whether liability is joint and several?
78. Are the amendment and waiver-of-rights provisions clear (e.g. what majority is needed to amend the SHA, and that a one-time waiver doesn't set a precedent)?
79. Is there a Successors and Assigns clause clarifying whether obligations bind heirs, personal investment holding vehicles, or transferees who haven't yet signed an accession?
80. Does the SHA state governing law and dispute resolution forum (state court vs. arbitration), and is it aligned with the same clause in the Articles?

## M. Power Balance — Control & Governance

81. Looking at board seats, reserved-matter vetoes, and quorum rules together, do investors hold effective control over *ordinary* business decisions — not just the extraordinary matters vetoes are meant to protect?
82. Do founders retain enough unencumbered board/voting control to run day-to-day operations without needing investor sign-off for routine, non-strategic matters?
83. Are investor board-appointment and veto rights tied to a dynamic ownership threshold that shrinks as their stake is diluted, or do these rights stay fixed ("sticky") even after their economic stake has fallen substantially?
84. Can any single party or a bare majority amend the SHA without full or unanimous shareholder consent — and could that be used to erode minority protections after signing?

## N. Power Balance — Economic Stacking & Misalignment

85. Layering the liquidation preference multiple, participation type, and anti-dilution mechanism together: in a *moderate* ("mid-outcome") exit scenario, do investors capture a share of proceeds meaningfully larger than their ownership percentage would suggest, at the founders' expense?
86. Is there a "missing middle" risk — if the liquidation preference is non-participating, is the conversion threshold set low enough that investors rarely have to convert, keeping most of the upside for a wider range of moderate outcomes than is typical?
87. Does the drag-along right, combined with liquidation preference stacking, let investors force a sale at a price that fully satisfies their own preference while leaving common shareholders with little or nothing — creating an incentive to sell early and modestly rather than aim for a larger outcome?
88. Considered cumulatively, do the protective provisions (board vetoes, liquidation stacking, anti-dilution, drag-along leverage) compound to a degree that would be considered off-market or one-sided for this stage and geography, rather than each being individually reasonable but collectively excessive?

## O. Power Balance — Reciprocity & Hidden Asymmetries

89. Do transfer restrictions (lock-up, RoFR, tag-along) apply symmetrically to both founders and investors, or are only founders restricted while investors face no equivalent constraint on exiting?
90. Could the board or management sell substantially all company assets (rather than shares) to route around the share-transfer protections (RoFR, tag-along, drag-along) that would otherwise apply?
91. Is there a Most-Favored-Nation clause benefiting certain investors only, and could it silently upgrade their terms in future rounds without equivalent protection or notice for founders?
92. Are there side letters or undisclosed preferential terms with specific investors that aren't reflected in the main SHA, creating unequal treatment among shareholders of the same class?

## P. Power Balance — Investor-Side Risks & Overall Fairness

93. Is there a related-party-transaction approval requirement that would catch founder self-dealing (e.g., diverting business to a founder-owned entity)?
94. Is there any restriction on the company issuing new debt or senior instruments that could rank ahead of the existing preferred shares in a liquidation?
95. Does the SHA give investors a consent right over future preferred share issuances, to prevent their existing liquidation preference from being structurally subordinated by a later round?
96. Does the company's reporting and information framework create sufficient management discipline and accountability to ensure that material issues, deviations from plan and emerging risks are identified and addressed early, rather than relying primarily on investor requests or contractual enforcement rights?
97. Is there an indemnification or liability provision that shields one side (founders or investors) from a specific risk without an equivalent protection for the other — an asymmetric risk allocation worth flagging?
98. Taken as a whole, does the SHA create a balanced framework that aligns founders, investors and other shareholders around long-term value creation, or do its governance, economic and protective mechanisms create material asymmetries or incentives that could lead one party to prioritise its own short-term interests over the company's long-term success?

## Q. Incentives & Alignment

99. Does the company's incentive structure align founders, key employees and investors around long-term value creation rather than short-term revenue, fundraising or exit objectives?
100. Are founder and key-employee incentives sufficiently meaningful to retain and motivate the people critical to the company's long-term success, while avoiding excessive dilution of existing shareholders?
101. Does the vesting, option-pool and leaver structure create appropriate incentives for founders and key employees to remain committed through the next financing round and beyond?
102. Do the economic incentives created by the SHA — including liquidation preferences, anti-dilution, drag-along rights and founder vesting — encourage shareholders and management to pursue value-maximising outcomes rather than premature or strategically suboptimal exits?

## R. Corroborating Evidence — Does Practice Match the SHA?

103. Does a signed Register of Shareholders exist that reconciles exactly with the SHA's stated cap table — including unvested and unexercised options — with a clean, unbroken chain of past share and option transactions and no unexplained gaps or unrecorded transfers?
104. Are signed minutes available for all Board meetings since the SHA was signed, evidencing that Important Board Matters were actually put to a vote and approved as the SHA requires — not merely discussed informally?
105. Are signed protocols available for all General Meetings of Shareholders since the SHA was signed, confirming that Important Shareholder Matters were approved with the quorum and majority the SHA requires — with no resolution passed in a meeting that failed to meet quorum?
106. Is there a complete summary of all warrants, convertible notes (SAFEs/CLAs), option grants, and any other instrument entitling its holder to obtain equity — including conversion triggers and resulting dilution — cross-checked against the SHA's anti-dilution and pro-rata subscription provisions to confirm nothing is missing or double-counted?
107. Are the findings from any prior investors' due diligence reviews available, and do they flag SHA-related issues (undisclosed side letters, governance breaches, unresolved disputes) that should already be reflected in — or that explain gaps in — the current SHA?
