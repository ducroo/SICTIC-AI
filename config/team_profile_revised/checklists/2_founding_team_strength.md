# 2 Founding-Team Strength

## Dynamics and cohesion

### Q001 / Q002 / R001 — Actual founding team and organisation

Q001, Q002, R001: Reconstruct the current organisation from the supplied evidence: active and former founders, stated equity holdings and roles, employees, reporting relationships and open positions. Does more than one active equity-holding founder have a substantive defined role, or does documented execution depend almost entirely on one person? Reconcile the deck's presentation with actual responsibilities and available organisation or employment records. Narrative voice alone is not proof that somebody runs the venture alone. Do not classify executives, employees, advisors or investors as founders without explicit evidence. Do not infer incapability merely from a solo-founder structure.

**Keywords:** founding team, cofounder, equity, cap table, organisation chart, employees, reporting lines, open positions

### Q003 / Q004 — Disclosed personal relationships and safeguards (core)

Q003, Q004: Are romantic or family relationships among active founders explicitly disclosed in the data room, and if so are any material implications for independent judgment, conflict handling or continuity addressed? Describe the actual relationship and safeguards only where stated. Do not infer relationships from names, photographs, locations or familiarity. A relationship is not itself evidence of poor team performance. If none is disclosed, use Insufficient information rather than asserting no relationship exists.

**Keywords:** cofounder relationship, family relationship, conflict handling, independent decisions, continuity, disclosed relationship

### Q016 / Q027 — Prior shared history and collaboration

Q016, Q027: What pre-existing shared work or other substantive history do active founders have, for how long, and what have they demonstrably accomplished together? Include shared employment at one company as one possible form of evidence. Distinguish overlapping dates or affiliation from evidence of close collaboration. Describe what the record supports about coordination; prior shared history alone does not establish interpersonal fit.

**Keywords:** founders shared history, previous colleagues, joint projects, collaboration, prior company, team cohesion

## Functional coverage and gap-closing

### N005 — Essential functional coverage (core)

N005: Derive the functions essential to the venture's next priorities and assess team capability and clear operating ownership for each: product/technical/domain execution; go-to-market (customer definition, positioning, pricing, channels and acquisition); sales (prospecting, pipeline development, enterprise relationships, negotiation and closing); operational delivery; and venture-specific functions such as regulatory, clinical, security, certification or marketplace operations. Always answer go-to-market, sales and supply chain / production separately. For hardware, cover relevant experience in design for manufacturing, suppliers, procurement/component availability, production cost, quality control, logistics, certification and scale-up. For each function report importance, named operating owner, whether that person is a founder, employee or contracted operator, their concrete relevant experience or achievements, and any material capability or capacity gap. Identify accountable founder oversight where documented, but an experienced employee or contracted operator can provide coverage; non-founder status is not a deficiency. Rate evidence coverage narratively as Covered (clear ownership and demonstrated capability), Partially covered (ownership with limited or transferable evidence), Uncovered (evidence establishes a material ownership/capability gap), Insufficient information (ownership or capability cannot be established), or Not applicable (function is not relevant to current priorities). Distinguish claimed achievements from substantiated outcomes, operating experience from titles, and active execution from advisor introductions. A pipeline, budget, planned hire or absent bill of materials does not by itself prove either skill or lack of skill. Keep this a people-capability assessment; discuss process or product evidence only when it establishes a specific team capability or execution dependency.

**Keywords:** functional coverage, product, go-to-market, sales, operations, manufacturing, supplier, procurement, bill of materials, quality, logistics, certification

### Q008 / Q009 / R010 — Recognition of gaps and concrete hiring plans

Q008, Q009, R010: For each material capability gap, does the team acknowledge it and present a substantive plan to close it? Assess a credible plan through its named interim operating owner, specific responsibilities and required capability, intended hire or external support, timing tied to a milestone, feasible budget or resourcing, and evidence of access to suitable candidates. Describe which of these are established and which are missing. Distinguish an executable plan from an unsupported statement such as "we will hire a salesperson"; a founder must still own customer discovery and initial execution where material. If actual hiring is documented, assess the relevant selection, onboarding and decision process. Do not require a formal HR process solely because financing is sought.

**Keywords:** skill gap, hiring plan, salesperson, recruitment, budget, candidate pipeline, onboarding, interim ownership

### Q131 — Limits of advisor support for a team gap

Q131: Where the active founding team has a material gap, does a named advisor or board member provide verifiable, relevant support that mitigates it? Identify the gap, specific contribution, evidence of activity, practical availability and remaining founder ownership. External advice can reduce a knowledge gap but normally cannot replace active responsibility for selling, customer discovery, product delivery or supplier management. State residual gaps; do not automatically upgrade functional coverage to Covered.

**Keywords:** advisor, board support, expertise, team gap, active contribution, availability, operating ownership

### N006 — Founder allocation to support functions

N006: Are active founders primarily dedicated to HR, finance, legal, administration or another support function while a material core execution function lacks ownership? Assess the actual venture and current organisational needs, the support role's immediate contribution, the core capability consequently missing, and whether external support or limited part-time responsibility could reasonably suffice. In a small founder-led venture, flag a material mismatch between founder allocation and execution needs when supported by evidence. Consider exceptions where the function is integral to the product or a principal execution risk, such as regulated financial infrastructure, legal technology or specialist regulatory delivery. A support-function title alone is not a deficiency.

**Keywords:** founder allocation, HR, finance, legal, administration, core execution, regulatory, support function

## Leadership, power balance and resilience

### Q117 — Alignment of operating responsibility and decision authority

Q117: Do the people accountable for material execution decisions have clear authority to make them, or do documented vetoes, competing mandates or unclear responsibilities create material conflict or deadlock? Identify the decisions, responsible active founders and any blocking structure. Majority equity and the CEO title need not sit with the same person; assess a concrete execution or accountability risk rather than title-equity symmetry.

**Keywords:** founder authority, decision rights, veto, accountability, deadlock, operating responsibility, control

### Q143 — Material concentration of execution dependencies

Q143: Is there an unusually concentrated, material dependency on one founder's exclusive access, knowledge, credentials or relationships, beyond the dependence expected for this organisation's size and work? Identify the specific critical activity or asset, the documented consequence of interruption and any practical mitigation such as shared access, documentation or a capable backup. Do not manufacture a concern from the generic observation that a small venture would struggle if its main founder were absent for 60–90 days. Do not require a formal succession plan.

**Keywords:** key person dependency, critical access, credentials, customer relationship, technical knowledge, backup, continuity

### R009 — Goal setting and performance management

R009: Are the documented goal-setting, accountability and performance-management practices proportionate to the actual team size and complexity? For a founder-only organisation, formal employee appraisals or peer-review systems may be Not applicable; do not require formal OKRs. Where employees or meaningful complexity exist, assess how goals, responsibilities, feedback and performance issues are handled, focusing on material effects on founder leadership and execution. Missing process documents alone do not establish poor performance.

**Keywords:** goals, accountability, performance management, feedback, employees, OKR, leadership, team size
