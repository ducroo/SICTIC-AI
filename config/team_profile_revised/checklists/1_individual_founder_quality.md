# 1 Individual Founder Quality

## Demonstrated capability and track record

### Q005 — Domain and technical capability (core)

Q005: Does at least one active founder have demonstrated domain or technical capability relevant to this startup's product and execution needs? Identify the founder, responsibilities, achievements and their relevance. Separate factual capability from titles and claimed expertise.

**Keywords:** founder, technical experience, domain expertise, product development, CV, resume

### Q006 — Business and commercial understanding (core)

Q006: Does at least one active founder demonstrate business and commercial understanding? Use concrete evidence of customer selection, positioning, pricing, distribution, acquisition economics or commercial decisions. A business degree or commercial title alone is insufficient.

**Keywords:** founder, business model, pricing, go-to-market, marketing, customer acquisition

### Q007 — Demonstrated selling experience (core)

Q007: Does at least one active founder have concrete evidence of selling something? Identify what was sold, to whom, the founder's own responsibilities and any documented outcome. Distinguish prospecting, negotiation and closing from marketing exposure or a company-wide sales claim.

**Keywords:** sales, founder, revenue, contracts, negotiation, customers, pipeline, closing

### Q025 — Experience in different operating environments

Q025: Does each active founder's experience consist entirely of large-corporate roles, or is there demonstrated execution with limited resources, uncertainty and personal ownership? Describe relevant transferable evidence. Corporate-only experience is a context and possible evidence gap, not an automatic deficiency; do not infer entrepreneurial ability from an employer name.

**Keywords:** corporate experience, startup experience, resourcefulness, founder, prior roles

### N001 / Q026 / Q028 — Individual founder traits (core)

N001, Q026, Q028: For each active founder, reuse the person profile's factual assessment of drive and agency, cognitive ability and learning, leadership and interpersonal effectiveness, grit and follow-through, and adaptability and range. Reconcile it against the retrieved evidence and identify missing support. Explain the facts, any contrary evidence and confidence for each trait separately. Educational achievements can supply relevant evidence but prestige alone does not establish ability. Do not estimate IQ or EQ, produce numeric scores or average founders together. If founder traits were not assessable in the profile, retain that uncertainty unless direct evidence resolves it.

**Keywords:** founder achievements, initiative, learning, leadership, perseverance, adaptability, education, projects, career progression

### R003 / Q135 — Demonstrated record and supporting credentials (core)

R003, Q135: What concrete, checkable evidence supports each active founder's claimed education, qualifications, prior responsibilities and achievements? Compare CVs, submitted biographies, certificates, employment records, supplied transcripts and references available in the data room. Identify material unverified claims and conflicting evidence. Missing certificates or references are Insufficient information for verification, not proof that a claim is false. Do not perform public verification or request outreach as an automated action.

**Keywords:** CV, resume, education, qualification, certificate, employment reference, founder achievement, transcript

## Fit with role and priorities

### Q011 — Ownership of the principal execution risk (core)

Q011: Does an active founder have the capability and operating ownership required for the startup's principal execution risk? First identify the most consequential venture-specific execution bottleneck to the next material milestone, deriving it from the startup profile and direct product, business, customer, regulatory, operational and financing evidence. Explain why this bottleneck dominates plausible alternatives in consequence and urgency. A generic risk such as competition or running out of money is insufficient without a concrete execution dependency. If no single risk can responsibly be identified, describe the candidates and uncertainty. Then identify the founder who owns it and the factual basis for their capability; an advisor's knowledge does not establish active founder ownership.

**Keywords:** execution risk, critical milestone, technical feasibility, customer acquisition, manufacturing, regulatory approval, founder ownership

### N002 / R002 — Founder responsibilities and role readiness (core)

N002, R002: For each active founder, identify the stated role, actual responsibilities, decision ownership, workload and work location. Derive the most important responsibilities from this specific venture and its next priorities. Provide Foundation (2–3 sentences on relevant demonstrated and transferable experience), Shortfalls (2–3 sentences distinguishing identifiable gaps from unknown information), and confidence with a reason. Compare the founder's evidence to the responsibilities rather than prior title repetition. A first-time CEO or CTO is not automatically unqualified. Do not produce a role-readiness score. Use supplied contracts and working arrangements where available; do not assume them from titles.

**Keywords:** founder, role, responsibility, workload, location, CEO, CTO, experience, operating priorities, employment agreement

## Commitment, availability and alignment

### Q014 — Founder compensation and financing dependence

Q014: Where the documents explicitly disclose founder compensation and its funding source, is compensation materially disproportionate to the venture's resources, revenue and planned work, particularly when a pre-revenue venture relies on a fresh raise to fund it? State the disclosed amounts, period, revenue context and source of funding. Do not infer salaries from the size of the raise or general projections. If compensation or funding source is not disclosed, use Insufficient information; do not call it excessive without a defensible comparison in the evidence.

**Keywords:** founder salaries, compensation, payroll, pre-revenue, fundraising, use of funds, budget

### Q021 — Conflicting commitments

Q021: Does any active founder have concurrent employment, another venture or obligations that could conflict with their startup responsibilities? Establish documented current commitments, availability and relevant restrictions. Distinguish an explicitly current role from an outdated biography. Preserve uncertainty and explain any demonstrated operational conflict.

**Keywords:** founder commitment, full-time, part-time, other employment, concurrent venture, conflicting obligations

### Q121 — Founder involvement in the assessment interaction

Q121: Does correspondence, an application record or a supplied meeting transcript establish that an active founder or CEO is personally and substantively involved in the assessment interaction? Identify the person and evidence. An application submitter name alone does not prove substantive involvement. If the interaction is not recorded in the data room, use Insufficient information without inferring disengagement.

**Keywords:** application, founder correspondence, CEO, meeting transcript, screening interaction, responses

### N003 — Personal commitment and exposure (core)

N003: What personal resources and opportunity costs has each active founder already committed before the proposed financing? Report disclosed cash, shareholder loans, guarantees, unpaid or below-market work, foregone work opportunities, personally contributed IP or equipment and other material exposure, with timing where given. Separate completed commitments from promises. Use CHF where stated or where a supplied conversion rate supports it; otherwise preserve the original currency and identify the missing conversion basis. Missing disclosure is not zero commitment. Do not reward wealth or rank founders by cash invested.

**Keywords:** founder investment, shareholder loan, guarantee, unpaid work, opportunity cost, intellectual property, personal contribution

### N004 — Duration of commitment and progress (core)

N004: When did work on the startup begin, when did each active founder join and become full-time, and what material milestones were achieved? Report approximate full-time and part-time founder-months only where dates and workload support them; state assumptions and do not invent part-time percentages. Compare elapsed effort with the difficulty and type of work and product, customer, commercial or financing progress. Conclude with too early to establish sustained commitment, proportionate progress, extended effort with limited progress, or Insufficient information, explaining the evidence. Use no universal month threshold.

**Keywords:** founder start date, full-time, part-time, milestones, progress, product launch, customers, financing, founder-months

## Self-awareness

### Q140 / Q141 — Response to feedback and self-assessment (core)

Q140, Q141: Do supplied correspondence, interview notes or transcripts show how active founders respond to critical feedback and discuss their own weaknesses? Identify substantive engagement, acknowledged limitations and resulting actions, or explicit evidence of deflection. Attribute statements to the individual founder and distinguish observed conduct from interpretation. Without recorded interactions, use Insufficient information; do not infer interpersonal traits from a polished deck or biography.

**Keywords:** critical feedback, founder interview, weakness, self-assessment, correspondence, transcript, response
